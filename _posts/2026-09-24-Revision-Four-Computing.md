This was the first programme I had undertook on EdX, and overall I was very happy with it. Stretching over thirteen modules it covered a range of topics including: data engineering pipelines and what the average job looks like in the abstract, relational, warehouse, and NoSQL databases, Python, Linux commands, and Apache Airflow and Apache Spark. I would say that each topic was rather shallow, the course creators I feel were going for breadth rather than depth in their teaching.

The format for each module was mostly a collection of five to ten minute videos, along with hands-on lab work to reinforce the theory from the videos. The quality of the videos is excellent, informative, concise, and easy to follow. The labs were even better, with IBM providing an online IDE from which instances of PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, Cassandra, Apache Airflow, Apache Spark, and Jupyter notebooks could be used. This was a fantastic resource to get to use the tools being taught without getting stuck on trying to create an environment locally for each of these technologies.

A lot of the material covered served as a refresher to knowledge I had forgotten, such as the Python, SQL, and relational database modules. What was fun was learning rudimentary Bash shell scripting and ETL with built-in Linux commands, as I had never gone further than simple CRON jobs for taking database backups before. The information heavy material on data warehouse theory and data engineering responsibilities was also engrossing.

I think that if the number of modules were to be halved, and instead more depth was given to those remaining, focusing on the practical Python, SQL, Linux commands and scripting, and Apache Airflow, there would be more to be gained from the program. As it stands it served as an introduction, informing me of technologies that should be studied more.
